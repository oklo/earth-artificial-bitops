# When does reversible computing become economically important?

The energy equation is:

```text
P = N_bitop kT ln(2) / eta_L
```

If artificial bitops demand keeps growing rapidly and `eta_L` saturates far below unity, irreversible computing eventually becomes a macroscopic energy consumer.  The point is not that reversible computing is necessary today; it is that the economics can change once the cost of irreversible bit erasure becomes a binding constraint.

In the central declining-growth model reconstructed here, the current global-energy-scale crossing occurs around `2090`.  In the constant 50%/yr bitops-demand sensitivity, the same crossing occurs around `2065`.

The current Earth-intercepted insolation crossing occurs later in the central model, around `2170`, but moves to around `2089` under persistent high growth.  Full solar luminosity is a civilization-scale or Dyson-scale marker rather than an Earthbound practical threshold; it is included because the talk has a distant-future framing.

These are sensitivity analyses, not forecasts.  They make the consequence of the assumptions auditable: rapid bitops growth plus bounded irreversible-computing efficiency eventually creates a large energy requirement.
