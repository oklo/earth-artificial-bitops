## 2. What was verified and holds up

### 2.1 Constants
- `k_B T ln 2` at 300 K = 2.871e-21 J. Correct (`src/earth_bitops/constants.py`).
- Earth-intercepted solar `π R⊕² S` = 1.735e17 W for S = 1361 W/m². Correct.
- `L_sun` = 3.828e26 W (IAU nominal). Correct.
- Global primary energy, 650 EJ/yr (20.6 TW). Slightly high. The IEA Global Energy Review 2026 reports +8 EJ (+1.3%) growth in 2025, which implies ~620–630 EJ; the Energy Institute substitution-method total is lower still. Effect on crossing years: under 1 year. Low priority.

### 2.2 Proof-of-work bucket (`current_model.py:95-97`, `historical_model.py:284-321`)
- **Hashrate.** The p50 of 9.8e20 H/s matches current reality. The network was ~1.0 ZH/s (7-day average) in late October 2026, after the first Q1 hashrate decline in six years (miners redirecting capital and power to AI hosting). The record single-day value was ~1.09 ZH/s (Sept 2025). CoinShares' 1.8 ZH/s year-end-2026 projection was conditional on a price recovery that has not clearly happened.
- **Fleet efficiency.** The model's 25 J/TH p50 is slightly pessimistic. J.P. Morgan put the network average at ~21.5 J/TH (Q3 2025) and ~20 J/TH (Dec 2025); the frontier is ~9.5 J/TH (hydro S23-class). At 20 J/TH, PoW power is ~22 GW instead of 27 GW, consistent with Cambridge's ~190 TWh/yr annualized survey figure.
- **Bitops per hash.** A gate-by-gate count of double SHA-256 with midstate (~1.9 compressions per nonce trial) gives:
  - 9.1e4 bitops/trial if a one-bit full-adder slice counts as one bitop (the repo's stated definition);
  - 2.4e5 if a full adder counts as ~5 two-input gate operations.

  The p50 of 1e5 matches the repo's own convention. Note that the inclusive headline scales linearly with this choice: a gate-level convention raises PoW (and AI) by ~2–3x.
- **Resulting PoW η_L:** 1.1e-5 to 1.3e-5 (wall), i.e. ~0.25 fJ per bitop. This is physically plausible for near-threshold, fully pipelined fixed-function logic.

### 2.3 AI accelerator bucket (`current_model.py:101-115`)
Independent bottom-up check:
- Installed stock: Epoch AI estimated >16.5M H100-equivalents (8-bit peak) in January 2026; its data-centre work extrapolates ~31.6M by September 2026; a third-party projection gives ~46M by end-2026.
- H100 8-bit dense peak 1.98e15 op/s, realized utilization 15–30%, 20–40 bitops per 8-bit op.
- Result: **1e23 to 1e24 bitops/s**. The model's AI p50 of 1.0e24 is at the top of this range: defensible, but on the high side.
- Site power: the model's 23 GW p50 is somewhat low. Epoch estimated ~30 GW of total AI data-centre capacity in late 2025, and the IEA counts 155 TWh (≈18 GW average draw) for AI-focused data centres in 2025, rising to ~465 TWh by 2030. A higher power input combined with the lower bottom-up bitops would lower the AI η_L slightly, to ~1e-7.
- Bitops per op: 64 (p50) is reasonable for BF16/FP8 MACs with wide accumulation. **However**, the industry move to FP8 and FP4 increases op/J but *decreases* bitops/op, because multiplier partial products scale with mantissa width squared. Vendor FLOP/J gains therefore overstate bitops/J gains. This should be modelled explicitly in any forward projection.

### 2.4 Other current-day buckets
Conventional data centres, telecom, PCs, phones and media are each 1e20–1e22 and individually immaterial to the inclusive total. The embedded bucket (`aggregate_bitops_per_s`, p10–p90 1e19–1e23) is a single four-decade placeholder; its mean (5.8e23) is tail-driven and would rival AI if used instead of the median. Flagged in the code itself; not re-litigated here. See §3.7 for its inconsistency with the historical series.

### 2.5 Per-capita columns of the talk table
Bitops/person/year and kWh/person/year were recomputed for 1940, 2025, 2030 and 2035 from bitops/s, η_L, kT ln 2 and UN population. All match to 3 significant figures. The arithmetic is not the problem; the inputs to the 2030/2035 rows are.

## 3. Findings (ranked by severity)

### 3.1 [Critical] Energy-crossing years are inputs, not outputs
`src/earth_bitops/future_model.py:46-89`

- `CENTRAL_SCENARIO.anchor_years = (2026, 2090, 2170, 2473, 2500)`, with `anchor_powers_w` set to exactly the three thresholds at those years. Power and η are then log-interpolated between anchors, so the "crossing years" found by `crossing_year()` are the anchor years by construction.
- `CONSTANT_50_SCENARIO` anchors 2065, 2089 and 2142 the same way, then back-solves η at those years so that 50%/yr bitops growth hits each threshold exactly.
- The docstring acknowledges this ("Calibrated to previous central declining-growth threshold crossings"), but the README presents the years as headline results.
- The "declining growth" is just the shape of the anchors. Recomputed from the output CSV, bitops growth is a constant 17.5%/yr for 2026–2090, 14.4%/yr for 2090–2170 and 9.0%/yr after that.
- `scripts/check_outputs.py:55-64` asserts these same years, so the test is tautological.

### 3.2 [Critical] Future η_L trajectories violate the no-reversible-computing premise
- Central: η_L = 4.5e-4 (2090) → 2.5e-3 (2170) → **0.25 (2473) → 0.30 (2500)**.
- Constant 50%/yr: η_L = **0.107 by 2065, 0.21 by 2089, 1.0 by 2160**.
- For irreversible logic, each operation must use a signal energy well above kT to keep thermal error rates acceptable. With p_err ~ exp(−E/kT):
  - p_err 1e-15 → E ≥ 35 kT → device η_L ≤ 0.020
  - p_err 1e-20 → E ≥ 46 kT → device η_L ≤ 0.015
  - p_err 1e-25 → E ≥ 58 kT → device η_L ≤ 0.012

  Wall-level η_L (cooling, power conversion, memory and interconnect, which dominate today) will be lower still. A realistic ceiling is ~1e-3. The scenarios therefore assume near-reversible efficiency inside an analysis whose premise is that reversible computing is unavailable.
- Consequence: the crossing years are *too late* for any given demand growth. Capping η_L at 1e-2 moves the 50%/yr crossings to 2059 / 2081 / 2135 (vs 2065 / 2089 / 2142); capping it at 1e-3 gives 2053 / 2076 / 2129.

### 3.3 [Critical] 2030 / 2035 talk-table rows are hard-coded and internally inconsistent
`src/earth_bitops/population_table.py:26-28`

- The values are literals. No code in the repo derives 1.89e27, 4.51e28, 2.29e-5 or 3.76e-4. The implied growth is 88.7%/yr in both 2025→2030 and 2030→2035.
- Comparison with the repo's own scenarios at the same years:

  | | Talk table | Central scenario | 50%/yr scenario |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2030 bitops/s | 1.89e27 | 1.98e26 | 5.3e26 |
  | 2035 bitops/s | 4.51e28 | 4.45e26 | 4.0e27 |
  | 2035 η_L | 3.76e-4 | 3.6e-6 | 2.1e-5 |

  The talk table exceeds even the "aggressive" sensitivity case by ~11x in 2035.
- **η_L plausibility:** 3.76e-4 in 2035 is ~34x better than today's average Bitcoin fleet (the most efficient bitops producer on Earth) and ~4000x better than today's AI fleet. Going from 1.4e-6 to 3.76e-4 is ~75%/yr η improvement for ten years. Historical Koomey-style improvement in energy per operation is ~1.3–1.5x/yr, and the precision effect in §2.3 works against bitops/J.
- **Likely origin:** the 88%/yr rate is close to the 2000→2025 log-average of the series, which is dominated by the one-off 2015→2020 ASIC proof-of-work ramp (~300x in PoW). Extrapolating it assumes another such transition every five years.
- **Plausibility from current data:** a two-component sketch (non-PoW from 1.35e24 and PoW from 1.0e26 at +10%/yr; see script section [5]):

  | non-PoW growth | 2030 total | 2035 total |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1.8x/yr (≈ IEA AI-power +30%/yr × 1.4x/yr bitops/J) | 1.6e26 | 5.0e26 |
  | 2.5x/yr | 2.0e26 | 5.4e27 |
  | 3.2x/yr (needed to match the talk table) | 2.9e26 | 4.8e28 |

  At ~1.4x/yr bitops/J improvement, 3.2x/yr bitops growth for nine years needs AI power to grow ~2.3x/yr: from ~30 GW to **~50 TW**, more than twice today's total world primary energy. Not credible.
- **PoW cannot carry it either.** The CSV allocates 98.3% of the 2035 total to PoW, i.e. ~450 ZH/s of Bitcoin. Miner revenue halves in 2028, and miners are currently shifting power to AI hosting.
- The talk-table text ("Extrapolated; likely SHA-256 PoW + AI…", "ASIC/accelerator compute likely dominant") is not supported by any calculation.

### 3.4 [High] Generated historical CSVs are corrupted for 2025 / 2030 / 2035
`scripts/run_historical.py:14-91`

- `append_talk_rows()` copies the full 2026 summary row as a template and overwrites only the bitops columns. As a result, 2025, 2030 and 2035 in `data/processed/earth_artificial_bitops_historical_summary.csv` carry **2026's power (1.83e11 W) and η_L (1.63e-6)**. The 2035 CSV row therefore has η_L = 1.63e-6 while the talk table says 3.76e-4: a 230x disagreement inside the repo.
- Non-PoW is set to `bitops / 60` (line 38-39, 56), imposing a 98.3% PoW share by fiat. This contradicts `docs/talk_table.md` ("not assigned to a detailed technology stack").
- The category CSV rows use placeholder `p50_associated_power_w = 1.0` and η values `1e-8` / `1e-6` (lines 67-83).
- The talk table's 2025 row (7.9e25, η 1.40e-6) is also hand-entered. Because PoW is 98% of the total and hashrate was ~1 ZH/s in both 2025 and 2026, the implied +32%/yr from 2025 to 2026 is unsupported.

### 3.5 [High] Stacked historical figure lost its category breakdown (packaging regression)
- `src/earth_bitops/historical_model.py` (byte-identical to `archive/.../earth_artificial_bitops_historical_model.py`) emits only two categories per year: "Non-PoW artificial computation" and "SHA-256 proof-of-work". `plotting.group_category()` maps the first to "Other computation".
- As a result, `figures/bitops_growth_stacked.png` shows one olive layer plus PoW, while the legend lists eight empty categories. The extrapolated 2030/2035 rows are also drawn into the stack as PoW.
- `archive/original_generated_files/earth_artificial_bitops_historical_categories.csv` (64 rows) and the archived figure *do* have per-category histories (early/legacy, mainframes/data centres, embedded, PCs, phones, telecom, media, AI, PoW). **The code that generated them is not in the repository**, so that breakdown is unreproducible.

### 3.6 [High] The inclusive η_L headline is a Bitcoin-share metric
- PoW η_L ≈ 1.1e-5; AI η_L ≈ 1e-7; all non-PoW combined ≈ 2.6e-8.
- Per GW: PoW yields ~4.5e24 bitops/s; AI yields ~3.3e22 bitops/s, a factor of ~140.
- Moving 1 GW from Bitcoin to AI therefore *removes* about 4.5e24 inclusive bitops/s, more than the whole AI sector produces today. With miners currently pivoting to AI hosting, inclusive bitops and inclusive η_L can **fall** while useful computation rises.
- The 2015→2020 "efficiency gain" in the inclusive series (3e-9 → 3.9e-7) is a mix shift, not a technology trend, and must not be extrapolated as one.

### 3.7 [Medium] Historical series is asserted, not derived
- `NON_POW_ANCHORS` (`historical_model.py:159-276`) hard-codes the p50 for every year. The Monte Carlo samples a lognormal around each p50 and returns it as the median, so the talk-table bitops column is the anchor list. The Hilbert & López, Koomey/Masanet and IEA sources are cited in notes, but no conversion (installed MIPS × utilization × bitops/instruction) is implemented.
- Spot check: Hilbert & López give 6.4e12 MIPS general-purpose capacity in 2007 (6.4e18 IPS installed). At ~5% utilization and ~300 bitops/instruction, that is ~1e20 bitops/s from general-purpose machines. The model's interpolated 2007 total of ~7e20 then needs application-specific/GPU logic to dominate. That is consistent with Hilbert & López's finding that application-specific capacity exceeds general-purpose capacity, but it is unverified in code.
- **Embedded inconsistency:** the archived category history has embedded/application-specific logic at ~5e22 in 2020, but the current-day model's embedded p50 is ~1e21 in 2026 — a ~50x drop in one category across six years with no explanation (visible in the archived figure as the orange layer collapsing after 2020).
- Minor: the 2010 PoW hashrate anchor of 1e10 H/s is ~10–100x low (the late-2010 network was ~1e11–1e12 H/s). It has no effect on totals.

### 3.8 [Medium] Thresholds are not physically comparable
- "Earth-intercepted solar power" is not an Earth-bound operating limit. Dissipating 1.74e17 W at the surface is ~340 W/m², raising the effective temperature by ~60 K before feedbacks. A meaningful intermediate threshold is about 1% of absorbed sunlight (~1.2e15 W ≈ 2.4 W/m² forcing, comparable to the present-day anthropogenic CO2 forcing).
- "Solar luminosity" only makes sense off-planet, where the radiator temperature (and so the kT used in the Landauer floor) would differ from 300 K. The model holds T = 300 K for all thresholds.
- In the constant-50% scenario, power reaches ~7e30 W by 2170 (>10⁴ L_sun) because η is pinned at 1 while demand keeps compounding. The scenario has no physical stopping condition.

### 3.9 [Low] Documentation and consistency nits
- README bullet: non-PoW η "≈2.4e-8"; README table: 2.56e-8. `check_outputs.py` accepts either (50% tolerance).
- Talk-table 2025 energy (173 kWh) uses η 1.40e-6; the CSV 2025 row implies 195 kWh (it uses 2026 power).
- `docs/sources.md` notes that several anchors were "inherited … should be rechecked" and were never rechecked.
- `historical_model.py` CLI default `--out-dir /mnt/data` is a leftover from the original environment.

## 4. Corrected framing for the energy extrapolation

A cleaner, assumption-explicit formulation that needs no hand-placed anchors:

```
P(t) = N(t) · kT ln2 / η_L(t),   N(t) = N0 (1+g)^(t−t0),   η_L(t) ≤ η_max
```

- With η_max = 1, the crossing year is a hard **latest-possible** bound for that demand growth.
- With η_max ≈ 1e-2 (device reliability ceiling) or 1e-3 (realistic wall ceiling), it gives the realistic crossing.

Recomputed (N0 = 1.04e26 in 2026). Each cell lists the crossing year for: global primary energy (630 EJ/yr) / 1% absorbed sunlight / Earth-intercepted sunlight / L_sun.

| g | η_max = 1 | η_max = 1e-2 | η_max = 1e-3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20%/yr | 2125 / 2147 / 2175 / 2293 | 2100 / 2122 / 2149 / 2267 | 2087 / 2109 / 2137 / 2255 |
| 30%/yr | 2095 / 2110 / 2129 / 2211 | 2077 / 2093 / 2112 / 2194 | 2068 / 2084 / 2103 / 2185 |
| 50%/yr | 2070 / 2081 / 2093 / 2146 | 2059 / 2069 / 2081 / 2135 | 2053 / 2064 / 2076 / 2129 |
| 88%/yr | 2055 / 2061 / 2069 / 2103 | 2047 / 2054 / 2062 / 2096 | 2044 / 2050 / 2058 / 2092 |

Interpretation for the talk: **the thesis survives and is sharper**. Under any growth rate ≥30%/yr and a realistic irreversible ceiling, computation reaches today's global energy budget this century (≈2055–2080), and reaches a climate-relevant waste-heat level (1% of absorbed sunlight) ~10–15 years later. Reversible or near-Landauer computing becomes economically decisive at that point, not at "η → 1".

Recommended η_L path (to replace hand-placed anchors): η_L(t) = η_max / (1 + (η_max/η0 − 1)·e^(−r(t−t0))) with η0 = 1.6e-6, r ≈ ln(1.3..1.5)/yr, η_max ∈ {1e-3, 1e-2}. Report the η_max = 1 bound alongside.
