# Earth Artificial Bit Operations

This repository estimates artificial computation on Earth from 1945 onward, expresses it as functional one-bit primitive operations per second ("bitops"), converts it to a Landauer-efficiency yardstick (bitops per erg), and asks when irreversible computing becomes a planetary and then astrophysical energy consumer if the historical trends hold.

All tables below are generated by `make all`. No table row or crossing year is hand-entered; see `docs/review_2026-11-11.md` for the adversarial review that led to the current structure.

> **Version 0.2.0 supersedes earlier results.** The 2030/2035 rows and all threshold-crossing years published in 0.1.0 were hand-entered and should not be cited. See `CHANGELOG.md` for the changes; the old state is kept at tag `v0.1.0`.

## Bottom line

**Today (2026).** About `1.0 x 10^26` bitops/s including SHA-256 proof-of-work, and `1.4 x 10^24` without it, on about 177 GW of wall power (0.9% of world primary energy). Wall-power Landauer efficiency is `1.7 x 10^-6` overall, but that is a blend of two very different machines: proof-of-work at about `1.3 x 10^-5` and everything else at about `2.6 x 10^-8`.

**The 80-year trend (non-PoW, 1945-2026).** Bitops grow about 62%/yr, Landauer efficiency improves about 44%/yr, and wall power grows about 12%/yr. The 2000-2026 window gives nearly the same efficiency trend (44%/yr) with slower demand (53%/yr) and power (7%/yr).

**Next decade (component projection, p50).** `1.6 x 10^26` bitops/s in 2030 and `4.2 x 10^26` in 2035 (p10-p90: `1.2 x 10^26` to `1.6 x 10^27`), on about 230 GW and 390 GW. Proof-of-work still supplies most bitops until the mid-2030s; AI takes over the power budget first (about 170 GW of the 2035 total) and has a 36% chance of passing proof-of-work in bitops by 2035.

**When computation becomes globally dominant.** If the trends hold, computation reaches today's world primary energy use (20 TW) in **about 2060-2070**. This date is robust: it is 2059-2070 across efficiency ceilings from 10^-3 to the Landauer limit itself, and 2063-2079 using the 2000-2026 trends. Before the ceiling, efficiency gains absorb most demand growth; once irreversible logic stops improving (around 2055-2075), power must grow as fast as demand, about 60%/yr instead of 12%/yr.

**When computation becomes astrophysically dominant (irreversible computing).** After the ceiling the remaining thresholds fall quickly:

| Threshold | Trends hold, irreversible (eta_max 10^-3 ... 1) | Power trend continues (requires reversible computing) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| World primary energy (20 TW) | 2059 - 2070 | 2070 |
| 1% of absorbed sunlight (waste heat becomes a climate forcing) | 2067 - 2081 | 2106 |
| All sunlight intercepted by Earth | 2077 - 2092 | 2150 |
| Solar luminosity | 2122 - 2136 | 2339 |

The right-hand column is the same demand trend with efficiency allowed to keep improving past the irreversible ceiling, which only reversible (sub-kT ln 2 per bitop) computing can do. The gap between the columns is the case for reversible computing: it buys about 25 years at the climate threshold, 60-70 years at the planetary threshold, and two centuries at the stellar one. Even a slow-demand case (+30%/yr) reaches world primary energy by 2085-2112.

These are conditional extrapolations, not forecasts. The Earth-sunlight and solar-luminosity rows are markers of scale: dissipating that power on Earth is not survivable, so the practical reading is that the trend must break, move off-planet, or go reversible before about 2070-2080.

## Reproduce

```bash
python -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -e .
make all PYTHON=python
```

`make all` runs: current-day model, historical model, 2026-2035 component projection, long-run extrapolation, figures, tables, then consistency checks. Monte Carlo seeds are fixed.

## Model definition

A bitop is a functional one-bit primitive operation implied by a workload: a Boolean operation, a one-bit adder slice, or a multiply partial product. It is not a FLOP, an instruction, a transistor-switching event, or a data-movement bit. Counting a full adder as about five two-input gates instead of one slice would raise SHA-256 and multiply-accumulate counts about 2.4x.

```text
eta_L = N_bitop k_B T ln(2) / P_wall        (T = 300 K)
P     = N_bitop k_B T ln(2) / eta_L
```

## Caveats

- Historical p50 values (1945-2020) are hand-set order-of-magnitude anchors with wide uncertainty; the Monte Carlo propagates their uncertainty but does not derive them. The fitted trends inherit this.
- The inclusive bitops and eta_L are dominated by proof-of-work after 2015. A gigawatt of mining yields about 100x the bitops of a gigawatt of AI, so inclusive totals can fall while useful computation rises. Use the non-PoW columns for trend arguments.
- The category split of non-PoW history comes from the original analysis bundle (`data/reference/`), whose generator was not preserved. "Embedded / application-specific" drops between 2020 and 2026 because the current-day model books GPUs and fixed-function logic under other categories.
- Non-AI, non-PoW wall power in 2026 (about 128 GW) is the 152 GW non-PoW anchor minus AI, not a bottom-up sum. The category model accounts for about 73 GW of it (conventional data centres about 35 GW, networks about 33 GW, device logic about 5 GW); the rest is implied device wall power.
- Long-run scenarios hold T = 300 K and hold proof-of-work at its 2026 level.
- The irreversible ceiling: reliable irreversible logic needs roughly 35-60 kT per operation, so device-level eta_L cannot exceed about 0.01-0.02; wall-level ceilings are lower. eta_max = 1 is shown as the hard latest-possible bound.

## File guide

- `src/earth_bitops/current_model.py`: 2026 product-form Monte Carlo by category.
- `src/earth_bitops/historical_model.py`: 1940-2026 anchors (non-PoW and proof-of-work).
- `src/earth_bitops/projection_model.py`: 2026-2035 component projection (proof-of-work, AI, other).
- `src/earth_bitops/future_model.py`: fitted trends, efficiency ceilings, threshold crossings.
- `src/earth_bitops/population_table.py`, `plotting.py`: derived tables and figures.
- `scripts/check_outputs.py`: consistency checks (energy equation, sums, cross-file agreement, monotonicity).
- `scripts/review_independent_checks.py`: standard-library recomputation used by the review.
- `data/processed/`, `figures/`: generated outputs. `data/reference/`: category shares taken from the archive.
- `docs/`: methodology, assumptions, sources, interpretation, and the review.
- `archive/original_generated_files/`: the original 0.1.0 bundle, deprecated and kept only for provenance (see `archive/README.md`).
- `CHANGELOG.md`: what changed between versions.
